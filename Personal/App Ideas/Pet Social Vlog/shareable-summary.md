# Pet Social Vlog - Product & Business Summary

## One-line

Pet Social Vlog is a daily companion app for active urban dog parents that turns dog walks, daily pet photos, and feeding routines into accountability, memories, and practical health insight.

## Short pitch

Dog owners already walk their dog, take photos, and worry whether they are doing enough. Today those behaviours are scattered across the camera roll, fitness apps, memory, chat messages, and occasional vet visits.

Pet Social Vlog brings them into one pet-centred daily loop:

1. Track the walk.
2. Capture pet photos and videos during the day.
3. Auto-generate a short daily or walk-end vlog.
4. Build a pet timeline.
5. Use activity history to guide routine, food, and health reports over time.

The product starts as a private memory and routine tool, then expands into household sharing, friend accountability, health intelligence, and later dog-walker service workflows.

## Target user

The first target segment is active urban dog parents:

- They own a dog.
- They live in a city or dense suburb.
- They walk the dog most days.
- They already take pet photos frequently.
- They care whether the dog is getting enough exercise, food, and attention.
- They are comfortable with daily habit apps, fitness trackers, or story-style sharing.

The product is not designed first for cat-only owners, passive pet viewers, or a public stranger-based pet social network.

## Core problem

Dog owners have three connected problems:

1. **Routine uncertainty** - "Am I walking my dog enough for this specific dog?"
2. **Memory overload** - pet photos pile up in the camera roll but rarely become something worth watching.
3. **Health guesswork** - feeding, weight, and vet conversations are often based on vague memory instead of structured logs.

## Product pillars

| Pillar | User job | Product answer |
|---|---|---|
| Routine Accountability | Walk the dog enough without guilt or guesswork | GPS dog walks, breed-aware activity targets, streaks, weekly progress |
| Curation Engine | Turn pet photos into something watchable | Daily auto-vlogs, walk recaps, pet-only timeline, year-in-review |
| Health Intelligence | Feed right and give the vet useful context | Food logging, activity-adjusted portions, weight/BCS tracking, vet PDF reports |

The same pet profile powers all three pillars: breed, age, weight, conditions, activity history, media history, and household context.

## Main features

### v1.0 MVP

- Pet profile for a single dog.
- Daily pet timeline for photos, videos, and notes.
- GPS walk tracking with route, distance, duration, and attached media.
- Breed-aware activity targets.
- Walk-end recap video.
- Daily auto-vlog export.
- Basic privacy controls: private, friends, public/export.
- Daily walking streak or weekly routine streak.
- Basic badge or milestone wall.

### v1.1 Social and household layer

- Household sharing for couples, families, or roommates.
- Friend invites.
- Private friend feed.
- Reactions such as paw, treat, or heart.
- Friends walking chart or leaderboard.
- Anti-double-walk awareness for households.

### v1.2 Health layer

- Quick-add food logging.
- Barcode and manual food entry.
- Activity-coupled portion recommendations.
- Anti-double-feed alerts.
- Weekly nutrition summary.
- Weight and Body Condition Score tracking.

### v1.3 to v1.4 Expansion

- Vet PDF report.
- Year-in-review.
- Diet Library with anonymised, opt-in dog diet references.
- More advanced collections, milestones, and pet customization.

### v2.0 Service layer

- Dog walker mode.
- Walker permissions controlled by the owner.
- Walk reports with route, photos, pee/poop notes, mood, water/food notes, and safety issues.
- Multi-dog walk support.
- Optional in-app payment or marketplace model, depending on traction.
- Wearables and widgets as companion surfaces.

## MVP loop

The smallest useful loop is:

1. User creates a pet profile.
2. User tracks a dog walk.
3. User captures or attaches pet media.
4. App generates a walk recap or daily vlog.
5. User saves or shares the result.
6. Weekly progress updates reinforce the next walk.

The MVP should validate that walking plus auto-vlog creation is strong enough before adding heavier social, health, or marketplace features.

## Business model

The current notes do not lock the final pricing yet. The recommended business model is a staged freemium model:

### Free consumer tier

Purpose: acquire users, prove the daily loop, and create organic sharing.

Likely includes:

- One pet profile.
- Basic walk tracking.
- Basic daily timeline.
- Limited auto-vlog templates.
- Watermarked exports.
- Basic activity progress.
- Basic privacy controls.

### Premium subscription

Purpose: monetize committed pet owners who use the app weekly.

Potential paid features:

- No-watermark exports.
- Premium vlog templates.
- Unlimited or higher-quality vlog exports.
- Year-in-review.
- Advanced breed-aware targets.
- Multi-pet support.
- Household sharing.
- Food logging and weekly health insights.
- Vet PDF reports.
- Advanced history search and archive.

### One-time purchases

Purpose: add non-essential revenue without blocking the core routine.

Potential items:

- Vlog template packs.
- Pet profile frames.
- Cosmetic pet accessories.
- Seasonal recap themes.

Cosmetics should stay ethical: no guilt messaging, no pressure loops, and no "your pet looks sad without premium" design.

### Dog walker service revenue

Purpose: create a higher-willingness-to-pay product line after consumer traction.

Possible models:

- Walker subscription for proof-of-walk reports.
- Owner-paid subscription for trusted walker access and report history.
- Per-walker seat pricing for small dog-walking teams.
- Optional transaction fee only if the product becomes a full marketplace.

This should not be in the MVP because it introduces scheduling, permissions, payments, trust, and support complexity.

### Future partnership revenue

Partnerships should come after product traction, not at launch.

Possible future areas:

- Vet advisory or clinic-facing report workflows.
- Smart feeder integrations.
- Wearable integrations.
- Pet food database licensing.

The Diet Library should not become an affiliate marketplace early; it should start as an anonymised reference and trust-building feature.

## Go-to-market wedge

Start with the behaviour users already do:

- Walk the dog.
- Take pet photos.
- Share cute moments.

The strongest early growth loop is watermarked auto-vlog export. A user gets a short, watchable pet recap and shares it to Instagram, TikTok, WhatsApp, or family chats. The share carries the product concept better than an ad because it shows the user's real pet and real routine.

Initial positioning:

> A daily health companion for active dog parents - walk enough, remember more, and understand your dog's routine.

## Competitive frame

Pet Social Vlog is not trying to be only one of these:

- Not just Strava for dogs.
- Not just Instagram for pets.
- Not just Google Photos for pet memories.
- Not just a food logger.
- Not a dog-walker marketplace at launch.

The defensible angle is combining dog-specific activity data, pet-only media curation, and health reporting around one pet profile.

## Retention mechanics

The product should bring users back through:

- Weekly walking progress.
- Streaks with forgiveness.
- Variable reward from daily auto-vlogs.
- Pet milestones and badges.
- Household accountability.
- Friend leaderboards after invite density exists.
- Year-in-review after enough history accumulates.

Retention should support healthy pet care, not addictive social pressure.

## Success metrics

Early beta targets:

- At least 40% of users complete onboarding, first walk, and first vlog on day 1.
- At least 25% are active on day 7.
- At least 50% of week-one walks include attached media.
- At least 20% export at least one vlog externally.
- Retained users average at least 3 walks per week.

If these fail, improve the core loop before adding more features.

## Key risks

- The auto-vlog quality must be high; bad daily recaps will train users to ignore the product.
- Food logging can become tiring; quick-add must be the default.
- Health recommendations create liability; advice must be framed as guidance, not diagnosis.
- Public social feeds have cold-start risk.
- Dog walker mode is attractive but can split focus too early.
- Premium pricing should not block the habit-forming core loop before retention is proven.

## Current open decisions

- Exact pricing: subscription, one-time purchases, or both.
- Whether free exports should be unlimited, watermarked, or capped.
- Whether premium templates are the first paid feature.
- Whether accounts are owner-centred or pet-centred.
- Whether leaderboards rank by distance, pet, household, or percentage of target.
- Whether dog walker mode becomes a private tool or full marketplace.
- Which health features require vet review before launch.
