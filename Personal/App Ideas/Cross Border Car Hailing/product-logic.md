# Product Logic

This note defines what information the app needs to understand before customer, driver, and backend implementation can be reliable.

## Product promise

Foreign customers should be able to book a legal, reliable cross-border car between Hong Kong and Mainland China without needing to understand local driver supply, border routes, payment norms, or language details.

Drivers should receive clear jobs with enough passenger, route, timing, pricing, and payment information to decide whether to accept and complete the trip.

## Core roles

| Role | Main responsibility |
|---|---|
| Customer | Searches a route, books a trip, pays, tracks the driver, receives support, and reviews the trip. |
| Driver | Open-marketplace supply partner who maintains eligibility, submits offers, executes trips, updates trip states, and receives payout. |
| Admin/Ops | Verifies drivers, configures pricing, monitors bookings, resolves disputes, and handles manual assignment. |
| Support agent | Helps with language, pickup problems, cancellation, refunds, border delay, and emergency cases. |

## Booking models

### Reservation booking

Reservation booking means the customer books in advance and the system has time to confirm driver availability.

Recommended flow:

1. Customer enters pickup, dropoff, date/time, passengers, luggage, vehicle class, and special needs.
2. System returns a fare quote or quote range.
3. Booking enters driver offer collection.
4. Eligible open-marketplace drivers submit offers.
5. System ranks eligible offers.
6. Customer sees up to the top 5 driver choices.
7. Customer selects one driver choice or rejects all choices and creates a new request.
8. Customer pays the selected fare by Stripe credit card in HKD.
9. Booking is locked to the selected driver after successful payment.
10. Driver executes pickup, border crossing, and dropoff.
11. After completion, driver payout is settled weekly by bank transfer outside Stripe.

Why this should be v1:

- Easier to support foreign customers.
- Easier to plan cross-border driver supply.
- Easier to handle border timing and route uncertainty.
- Lower risk than instant booking if driver density is not high yet.

### Instant booking

Instant booking means the customer expects a nearby driver now.

It needs:

- Reliable live driver availability.
- Accurate pickup ETA.
- Clear eligibility filtering before the driver receives the request.
- Surge or shortage rules.
- Fast cancellation and fallback logic.
- Strong support process if the driver cannot cross the selected route.

Do not make instant booking the only v1 model unless driver supply is already strong on target routes.

### Bidding model

Bidding means the customer posts a trip request and drivers submit prices or terms.

Possible use cases:

- Long-distance or unusual routes.
- Premium vehicles.
- Trips outside standard service areas.
- Low-supply time windows.
- Corporate or high-value bookings.

Rules needed before shipping bidding:

- Driver bid price is not controlled by the platform in v1.
- Whether bids include tolls, parking, waiting, border-related fees, and luggage.
- Bid expiry time.
- Whether the customer can negotiate.
- Whether drivers can see competing bids.
- Whether the platform can auto-filter low-quality bids.
- What happens if the chosen driver cancels.
- Whether the platform fee is included in the bid or added later.

Confirmed v1 position: bidding is customer-visible, but filtered. The customer should not see every bid. The system should show the top 5 choices from eligible open-marketplace drivers.

Drivers set their own offer price. The platform does not enforce a minimum or maximum bid price in v1, but the platform still filters by driver eligibility, route capability, document status, service quality, and fraud/safety controls before an offer can be shown.

### Driver current-area eligibility

Drivers can be approved to serve both directions, but they should only receive an order if they are currently in the pickup-side area.

Examples:

- If the driver/car is currently in Hong Kong, the driver can receive Hong Kong-to-Mainland orders.
- If the driver/car is currently in Shenzhen or Guangzhou, the driver can receive Mainland-to-Hong-Kong orders from that city/area.

The backend should decide this from current driver location or current declared service area. The mobile app should not rely only on the driver's manual selection.

### Customer-visible top 5 choices

The top 5 list should be generated by backend ranking rules, not by frontend sorting.

Each customer-visible choice should show:

- Final customer price.
- Vehicle class and vehicle photo.
- Driver rating and completed trip count.
- Estimated pickup readiness or scheduled availability.
- Included fees and excluded fees.
- Luggage/passenger fit.
- Driver language support if available.
- Cancellation rule summary.

Confirmed ranking direction: use a balanced value score that combines the quality, price, timing, and vehicle-fit signals instead of ranking only by cheapest price.

Suggested ranking model:

1. Driver and vehicle eligibility.
2. Route and border capability.
3. Driver reliability score and past cancellation rate.
4. Bid price relative to reference fare.
5. Vehicle fit for passenger and luggage needs.
6. Scheduled availability or expected wait.
7. Customer language fit.
8. Platform margin and quality controls.

Suggested v1 scoring weights after hard eligibility filters:

| Signal | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Driver reliability and completed trips | 30% |
| Price/value against reference fare | 25% |
| Vehicle fit and service class | 20% |
| Availability/wait confidence | 15% |
| Language/support fit | 10% |

Do not rank only by lowest price. For foreign customers, reliability and clarity matter more than a small price difference.

### Offer collection window

Drivers have two minutes to submit offers after a customer creates a reservation request. After two minutes, the backend generates the top 5 offer set from eligible offers.

If fewer than five eligible offers arrive within two minutes, the app shows fewer choices. If the customer refuses all available choices, the customer can create a new request and restart the offer collection window.

## Fare and pricing engine

The pricing engine is a backend rule system, not just a UI calculator.

### Inputs

- Pickup location.
- Dropoff location.
- Trip direction: HK to Mainland and Mainland to HK.
- Scheduled pickup time.
- Booking type: reservation, instant, bidding.
- Vehicle class.
- Passenger count.
- Luggage count and luggage size.
- Estimated distance.
- Estimated duration.
- Border crossing route.
- Tolls, bridge fees, parking fees, and waiting rules.
- Driver availability.
- Demand/supply pressure.
- Customer discount or campaign.
- Currency: HKD.

### Output

- Customer displayed fare.
- Driver expected earning.
- Platform fee.
- Stripe charge amount.
- Cancellation fee.
- Waiting fee rules.
- Extra charge rules.
- Refund policy.
- Quote expiry time.

### Normal fare calculation

Example structure:

```text
fare =
  base_fare
  + distance_km * per_km_rate
  + estimated_time_min * per_min_rate
  + tolls_and_route_fees
  + border_service_fee
  + vehicle_class_fee
  + luggage_or_special_request_fee
  + demand_adjustment
  - reservation_discount
```

The final formula should be configurable by route, vehicle class, booking type, and time window.

### Reservation discount

Confirmed rule: reservation is 20% cheaper than the instant booking reference fare.

```text
reservation_fare = instant_reference_fare * 0.8
```

The backend should still store this as a configurable discount rule by route and time window, not as a hard-coded mobile rule.

## Dispatch and driver matching

Driver matching should use eligibility before proximity.

### Driver eligibility checks

- Driver account verified.
- Vehicle verified.
- Required cross-border documents verified.
- Driver can serve the trip direction.
- Driver is currently in the pickup-side area for the order.
- Driver can serve the selected border route.
- Driver has enough capacity for passengers and luggage.
- Driver is available at the requested time.
- Driver has no conflicting booking.
- Driver rating or service quality is above threshold.
- Driver supports required customer language, if needed.
- Driver is allowed to receive the selected booking type.

### Matching priority

Recommended priority order:

1. Legally and operationally eligible.
2. Available for the requested pickup time.
3. Route and border capable.
4. Good service quality.
5. Reasonable ETA or location.
6. Bid price and expected platform margin.
7. Driver marketplace quality controls.

For reservation booking, dispatch can be slower and more quality-focused. For instant booking, dispatch must be faster but should still reject ineligible drivers before showing the job.

## Booking state machine

The backend should own booking state. Customer and driver apps should render state, not invent it.

Recommended states:

```text
draft
quote_generated
quote_expired
collecting_driver_offers
offers_ready
customer_selecting_driver
driver_selected
payment_pending
payment_authorized
payment_paid
payment_failed
driver_confirmed
customer_confirmed
driver_en_route
driver_arrived
trip_started
border_crossing
trip_completed
settlement_pending
settled
cancelled_by_customer
cancelled_by_driver
cancelled_by_admin
refunded
disputed
```

Each state needs:

- Who can move it forward.
- Who can cancel.
- What notifications are sent.
- What payment action happens.
- What support action is available.
- What data is locked from editing.

## Cross-border trip rules

This product has more constraints than normal city ride hailing.

Information to collect:

- v1 routes: Hong Kong airport to Shenzhen, Hong Kong city to Shenzhen, and Hong Kong to Guangzhou.
- Allowed directions: both Hong Kong to Mainland and Mainland to Hong Kong.
- Hong Kong service area: every Hong Kong pickup/dropoff area.
- Mainland service cities: all Shenzhen and Guangzhou areas for v1.
- Allowed border crossing points: all supported cross-border vehicle crossings for v1, stored in backend configuration.
- Estimated border processing time by crossing and time window.
- Whether airport, hotel, exhibition center, and residential pickups are all supported.
- Vehicle and driver permit requirements.
- Insurance requirements.
- Whether children, pets, large luggage, wheelchairs, or business equipment are allowed.
- What happens if border crossing is delayed or rejected.

Do not hard-code these rules in the mobile app. Keep them in backend configuration so operations can update them without app releases.

## Driver onboarding and compliance

Driver supply can include both individual driver accounts and fleet/company accounts that manage multiple drivers.

Driver/vehicle verification should collect the full document set before a driver can receive cross-border jobs:

- Driver identity document.
- Driving license.
- Vehicle license/registration.
- Cross-border permit.
- Insurance.
- Business registration or fleet/company registration when applicable.

Kevin's team will confirm the exact legal names of the required permits before implementation. Until then, the product should assume all core driver, vehicle, cross-border, insurance, and business documents are required.

## Driver cancellation policy

Driver cancellation after accepting a selected booking should be recorded and penalized by timing.

Rules:

- If the driver cancels within 5 minutes after accepting, allow cancellation but log the event.
- If the driver cancels more than 5 minutes after accepting, apply a warning/penalty record.
- If the driver exceeds 3 late-cancellation penalties, temporarily ban the driver from receiving orders for 5 hours.

The backend should track late-cancellation penalties over a weekly rolling window.

## Payment, refund, and settlement

Recommended v1:

- Customer pays by credit card only.
- Fare is displayed and charged in HKD.
- Stripe merchant entity should be the Hong Kong company.
- Reservation bookings use Stripe PaymentIntents to charge the full fare at booking.
- Instant bookings use Stripe PaymentIntents with manual capture: authorize/hold first, then capture after ride completion.
- Reservation booking is confirmed only after payment succeeds.
- Instant booking is confirmed only after payment authorization succeeds.
- If the customer cancels more than 24 hours before pickup, refund 85% and keep 15% as the cancellation fee.
- Reservation cancellation within 24 hours keeps 15% and refunds 85%.
- Reservation cancellation more than five minutes after driver arrival keeps 30% and refunds 70%.
- Instant cancellation is free within 10 minutes after driver acceptance.
- Instant cancellation after 10 minutes captures 10% if driver ETA is 10 minutes or less; otherwise release the hold.
- Instant booking after-arrival cancellation captures 10% if the driver has arrived and the customer cancels after a five-minute grace window.
- Instant cancellation fee split is 70% driver and 30% platform.
- Reservation after-arrival retained fee split is 70% driver and 30% platform.
- Admin can override refund and extra charge decisions.
- Platform commission is deducted as 10% of the driver's submitted price; it is not added on top of the customer-visible fare.
- Every Friday, driver payout covers all eligible completed rides since the previous Friday and is paid by bank transfer outside Stripe after admin/system settlement.

See [[payment-and-cancellation]] for the detailed Stripe flow and cancellation rules.

## Customer trust and support logic

Because the target customers are foreigners, the app should reduce uncertainty.

Needed logic:

- Multi-language onboarding and booking copy.
- International phone number support.
- Hotel/airport pickup instructions.
- Driver identity and vehicle display.
- Live trip status.
- In-app support handoff.
- In-app customer-driver/support chat.
- Support can join the active customer-driver chat.
- Support joins when the customer or driver requests help.
- Support operating hours: 09:00 to 00:00.
- Button-triggered auto-translation in chat.
- Emergency contact workflow.
- Clear cancellation and refund policy before payment.

Support SLA:

- Active-trip support response target: under 5 minutes.
- V1 support channel: in-app chat.
- V1 support coverage: operating hours only, 09:00 to 00:00.
- V1 staffing: admin staff, initially the co-founders or staff from Kevin Tsang.

## Driver customer-info visibility

Before the customer accepts and pays:

- Driver should not see customer language.
- Driver should only see operational booking details needed to price the offer.

After the customer accepts and payment succeeds:

- Driver can see the customer information required to execute the ride.

## Admin operations

The product needs an admin system even if the customer and driver apps are the visible interfaces.

Minimum admin capabilities:

- Driver and vehicle verification.
- Document expiry tracking.
- Fare rule configuration.
- Service area configuration.
- Marketplace offer monitoring.
- Top-5 ranking override or suppression.
- Booking timeline view.
- Payment/refund override.
- Support ticket management.
- Driver quality flags.
- Customer complaint handling.
- Audit log for sensitive changes.

## Analytics and metrics

Track business logic, not only page views.

Core metrics:

- Quote created.
- Quote accepted.
- Payment started.
- Payment completed.
- Booking confirmed.
- Driver assigned.
- Driver offer submitted.
- Top 5 offers shown.
- Driver choice selected.
- Driver accepted.
- Driver cancelled.
- Customer cancelled.
- Pickup success.
- Border delay.
- Trip completed.
- Refund requested.
- Dispute opened.
- Repeat booking.

These events should include route, booking type, vehicle class, customer locale, payment method, and driver assignment method.
