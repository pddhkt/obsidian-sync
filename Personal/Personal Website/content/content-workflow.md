# Content Workflow

## Goal

Research once, keep the source thinking in Obsidian, and adapt it into several useful public formats without forcing every idea into a full article.

## Step 1: Capture

Put raw material into [[Personal/Personal Website/content/research-inbox/_readme|Research inbox]]:

- A question you want to answer.
- A lesson from a project.
- A useful link with your reaction.
- A concept you want to understand.
- A screenshot, quote, or note from daily work.

Keep the first capture short. The point is not to lose the idea.

## Step 2: Research and brainstorm

When an idea is worth developing, create a note in `topics/` using [[Personal/Personal Website/content/templates/topic-research-template|Topic research template]].

Separate:

- What you already know from experience.
- What needs external research.
- Your opinion or working hypothesis.
- Useful examples.
- Open questions.

## Step 3: Choose the content angle

Create a brief using [[Personal/Personal Website/content/templates/content-brief-template|Content brief template]].

Decide:

- Who the content is for.
- What problem it helps with.
- The one useful takeaway.
- Whether it should become a blog, short post, tutorial, project note, or several formats.

## Step 4: Draft the long-form source

Write the blog draft or canonical note in `drafts/`. It does not need to be long. A useful short note is better than padding.

## Step 5: Break into social variants

Use [[Personal/Personal Website/content/templates/social-post-template|Social post template]].

Extract:

- One concise post with the main lesson.
- One personal observation.
- One practical checklist or example.
- One link back to the blog when the blog is published.

Adapt the tone to the platform instead of pasting identical text everywhere.

## Step 6: Publish and log

Record:

- Published URL.
- Date.
- Channel.
- Source topic note.
- Related project.
- What to improve later.

Store the log in [[Personal/Personal Website/content/published/_readme|Published content]].

## Content quality check

- [ ] Is there one clear takeaway?
- [ ] Is the intended reader clear?
- [ ] Is the content based on experience, research, or both?
- [ ] Are private work details removed?
- [ ] Does the post say something more useful than a generic summary?
- [ ] Can one part become a social post?
